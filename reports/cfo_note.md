# Note to the CFO: where Baraka makes and loses profit, 2023–2025

**From:** Financial Analyst, FP&A · **Source:** Baraka Financial Performance report (`BarakaFinance.pbip`), finance system extract, January 2023 – December 2025. All values in KES.

Over three years Baraka sold **KES 301.0M** after discounts, made **KES 77.8M profit (25.8% margin)** and gave away **KES 15.7M in discounts**. Sales grew 5.8% in 2024 and 7.7% in 2025, but the margin slipped from 26.9% to 25.0% and the business has run behind plan since January 2024.

## Three findings

1. **Supermarkets earn the most profit; small shops earn the best margin.** Supermarkets made **KES 35.6M**, 46% of all profit. Small shops have the highest margin, **32.3%**, and grew fastest, **+16.0%** from 2023 to 2025. Distributors have the lowest margin, **17.0%**.
   *Report: Profit drivers → "Total profit by segment" and "Profit margin by segment".*

2. **Discounts cost the most margin in the High band, and Distributors take half of all discounts.** Margin falls from **31.0%** on undiscounted sales to **13.8%** in the High (15%) band. It drops by 11–14 points within every segment. Distributors receive **49.5% of all discounts** and put **40% of their sales** through the High band, where their margin is just **11.2%**. High-discount export sales earn only **8.1%**, and all 18 loss-making rows are High-discount exports.
   *Report: Profit drivers → "Profit margin by segment and discount band" matrix and "Where the discounts go".*

3. **The business has missed plan since January 2024, and Kigali is the weakest market.** Sales beat plan by 2.1% in 2023, then fell short in **all 12 months of 2024 (−2.1%)** and 8 of 12 months of 2025 (−0.3% for the year). Kigali is **17.1% behind** its allocated target in 2024 and **14.3%** in 2025. Its sales dropped about 22% year on year from July 2024, and it only returned to growth, from that lower base, in July 2025. Kigali's 2025 sales are still 17% below 2023. Every other market is within 3% of plan.
   *Report: Markets and plan → "Actual sales against target by month" and "Variance to target by market".*

## Three recommendations

1. **Give Small shops more of next year's budget.** They combine the highest margin (32.3%), the lowest discount rate (3.2% of gross sales) and the fastest growth (+16.0% since 2023), and they beat plan in 2023 and 2025. Pair this with **Mango Juice**, the fastest-growing product (**+139.7%** since 2023) and the most profitable per shilling (**35.3% margin**).
   *Evidence: Profit drivers → "Profit margin by segment" and the Products table.*

2. **Tighten the discount policy.** Cap distributor discounts at Medium (7%) unless the CFO approves a deal, and stop High (15%) discounts on exports. Had the distributors' High-band sales gone at 7% instead of 15% at the same volume, profit would have been about **KES 2.9M higher over three years** (about KES 1M a year). Some volume may be lost, so trial the cap with the largest distributors first and track units sold.
   *Evidence: Profit drivers → discount band matrix and "Where the discounts go".*

3. **Set up a Kigali recovery review and plan by market.** Find out what changed in July 2024 (a lost customer, a distributor, pricing or a border issue) and agree a recovery target. For the 2026 budget, set targets by **market as well as segment**, so shortfalls show up in the month they start rather than in a year-end variance.
   *Evidence: Markets and plan → "Variance to target by market" and "Sales and profit by market".*

**Limits.** Targets exist only by segment and month. Market targets are an allocation based on each market's prior-year share, so treat them as a guide. The discount savings estimate assumes volumes would not change.

*Data: Baraka Manufacturing is fictional; the data is synthetic, generated to follow the layout of Microsoft's Financial Sample workbook (DSEAfrica Data Lab).*
